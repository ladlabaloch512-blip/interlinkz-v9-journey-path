How to Choose a Website for a Small Business

Interlinkz Blog · 17 November 2026

Start with what your business needs

Every business needs something different from a website. A shop may need an online store. A clinic may need clear service pages and an easy way to book. A local service business may only need a few strong pages and a contact form. Before you choose anything, write down the main job your website must do. That one job should guide every other choice.

It also helps to think about your visitors. Most of them will arrive on a phone, often from a social media link or a search. Your website has to load fast and make the next step obvious, whether that step is calling you, sending a message or placing an order.

Choose pages before design

A clear list of pages keeps the project simple and the price clear. Most small businesses start with a home page, a services page, an about page and a contact page. Stores add product and checkout pages. Clinics and services often add booking or appointment pages.

Once the pages are listed, look at designs. A good agency shows you a design before the build starts, so you can see the layout, the colours and the style and approve them first. Nothing about the final site should be a surprise.

The right website is the one your customers can use easily and you can update without stress.

Ask about updates and support

A website is not finished on the day it goes live. Prices change, photos change and new services appear. Ask how updates will be handled and what they cost before you start. Some owners want to update pages themselves, so the site must be easy to edit. Others prefer to send changes to the agency. Both are fine, as long as the route is clear from the start.

Also ask what happens if something breaks. A clear support route, even a simple email address that a person reads, saves a lot of stress later.

Compare the full picture, not only the price

The cheapest quote is not always the cheapest website. Check what is included: the design, the number of pages, the contact form, the setup on your domain and hosting, and help after launch. A slightly higher quote that includes these things can cost less over a year than a low quote that adds a fee for every small thing.

Choose the team that explains things in plain words and shows you real examples of their work. If you can see demo websites working before you order, you already know what you are buying.
